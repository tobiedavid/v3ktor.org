V3KTOR

Four free tools for running experiments on yourself. You’re the subject and the observer. Start with the question that sounds like yours.

Which foods set my gut off?

Food Trial Log. Log meals, stools and symptoms. Take one suspect food out, bring it back, and compare.

Open the logEntries logged in English won’t appear in the French version, and vice versa.

Why do I keep crashing?

The Patch. Log meals, sleep, stimulants and symptoms, then compare your better days with your worse ones.

Open The PatchRead the introduction first

Where does my vicious circle start?

Sollum. Tell Sollum what you want to stop or keep an eye on. In about two minutes, tap which matters more: Tomorrow or Right now, Family or Being left alone. It learns your normal, and with the incidents you report it draws your circle, so you can try to break it before you’re too far in.

One bad day tells you nothing. Fourteen might.

These tools keep the record so you can look across days. They don’t tell you what it means.

How it works

  1. NoticeWrite down what happened and when.
  2. CompareLook across days or sessions for what repeats, and what doesn’t.
  3. DecideYou choose what’s worth looking into next.
  • Free to use
  • No account
  • Open source

Not medical advice. These tools support personal observation. They cannot confirm a diagnosis or replace medical care. Seek professional advice for persistent, severe or concerning symptoms.

Food Trial Log, The Patch and Sollum keep your entries in your browser’s storage, on your own device. There is no account, and nothing you enter is sent anywhere. Clearing browser data or changing devices erases them, so keep a backup where the tool offers one.
