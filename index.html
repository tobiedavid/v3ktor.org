Five free tools for running experiments on yourself. You’re the subject and the observer. Start with the question that sounds like yours.

Which foods set my gut off?

Food Trial Log. Log meals, stools and symptoms. Take one suspect food out, bring it back, and compare.

Open the logEntries logged in English won’t appear in the French version, and vice versa.

Why do my negative effects seem random?

Threshold Log.

  • Each compound in food has its own bucket. Fill it past the rim and a negative effect shows. Your buckets aren’t the same size as anyone else’s.
  • Foods that look unrelated fill the same bucket. Cheddar, salami and red wine all pour in tyramine.
  • A compound judged safe can still overflow yours.

Report your meals and your negative effects. The app tracks 25 compounds across your meals, and shows how full each bucket was when an effect came.

Open the Threshold LogOn its own for a problem that comes and goes. After the Food Trial Log for one that’s there most days.

Why do I keep crashing?

The Patch. Log meals, sleep, stimulants and symptoms, then compare your better days with your worse ones.

Open The PatchRead the introduction first

Where does my vicious circle start?

Sollum. Tell Sollum what you want to stop or keep an eye on. In about two minutes, tap which matters more: Tomorrow or Right now, Family or Being left alone. It learns your normal, and with the incidents you report it lays out the chain that led to each one, link by link, so you can try to break the circle before you’re too far in.

How it works

  1. NoticeWrite down what happened and when.
  2. CompareLook across days or sessions for what repeats, and what doesn’t.
  3. DecideYou choose what’s worth looking into next.
  • Free to use
  • No account
  • Open source

Not medical advice. These tools support personal observation. They cannot confirm a diagnosis or replace medical care. Seek professional advice for persistent, severe or concerning symptoms.

Food Trial Log, Threshold Log, The Patch and Sollum keep your entries in your browser’s storage, on your own device. There is no account, and nothing you enter is sent anywhere. Clearing browser data or changing devices erases them, so keep a backup where the tool offers one.
